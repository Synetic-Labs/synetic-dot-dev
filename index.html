We do research and development of computational systems, specializing in machine intelligence for embodied systems and autonomous control in advanced robotics and uncrewed vehicles. We build hardware and offer software development consulting services.

■ Research Projects

Project Navatar High-speed autonomous drone racing using state-of-the-art deep reinforcement learning. World models, soft actor-critic and behavioural cloning train the drone efficiently in simulation to perform superhuman actions in the real world.
→ learn more
Project Starling Smarter drone swarms for autonomous navigation, following, tracking and targeting, built from sophisticated algorithms, advanced connectivity and practical hardware. More intelligent swarms multiply force with fewer human pilots.
→ learn more
Project Argus Detecting and identifying objects in the sky from photons alone, with a simple, low-cost multi-camera setup and advanced computer vision and image processing.
Project Quail An agile FPV drone built to be the ideal platform for autonomous drone research: safe to fly indoors, expandable with sensors, and ready for an autonomous flight controller. Aiming to be a fully open source, digital-video hardware product.
Project Snow Owl A drone designed to operate in extreme cold, with research into ice-wall perching, mesh communication and illumination, and keeping batteries and control boards alive at very low temperatures. Specifically being researched for search and rescue missions, and crevasse traversal.

■ Open Source Software

SkyFlow A fast quadrotor simulator in JAX, for training thousands of drones at once. Wind, command delay, ground contact and crashes are part of the world. A policy can fly through the real firmware, by way of cudaflight. Over a billion physics steps a second on one RTX 4090.
→ source ・ MIT
SkyFlow Dynamics Every force, torque and motor model for multirotors that we know of, written once as symbolic math with its source. Each is tested two ways: against physics that must hold, and against numbers from running the reference simulators themselves (RotorPy, Crazyflow, SkyDreamer, JSBSim). The JAX code is generated from the math, not written by hand. Its 5-inch racer is fitted to public flight data from the University of Zurich's NeuroBEM and Agilicious quad.
→ source ・ MIT
cudaflight Real Betaflight firmware, thousands of instances on one GPU: one thread is one complete flight controller. Training sees the same PID loops, rates, filters, mixer and OSD that fly the real aircraft, and can start from a real quad's settings dump. The firmware itself is unchanged, and the fork follows Betaflight's releases.
→ source ・ GPL-3.0
Analog Vision Analog NTSC video over an FM radio link, simulated from the signal up: what the camera on an analog FPV drone sends, and what the pilot's goggles show, at any signal strength. Nothing is painted on. Grain, sparklies, tearing and snow all come out of the chain.
